title: Round Table Conferences and the Poona Pact
type: Curator note
date: 1930–1932
place: London; Poona
---
Ambedkar represented the depressed classes at the Round Table Conferences in London between 1930 and 1932, arguing for political safeguards including separate electorates.

The British government's Communal Award of 1932 granted separate electorates to the depressed classes. M. K. Gandhi opposed this and began a fast in Yerwada jail in Poona. After negotiations, Ambedkar and Gandhi's side agreed to the Poona Pact on 24 September 1932, which replaced separate electorates with seats reserved for the depressed classes within the general electorate.

The Poona Pact remains one of the most debated episodes of the period, and reserved representation later carried into the Constitution.
